The problem

The agency's only web presence was a corporate page it didn't control. Every client came by referral, and there was nowhere to send someone who wasn't ready to pick up the phone yet.

What I built

  • The whole site — nine product pages, quote form with California ZIP validation, life insurance needs calculator, exit-intent offer
  • A Google Apps Script endpoint that catches every submission and writes it to a live sheet, tagged by which email signature or form it came from
  • A ManyChat life insurance flow handling the conversation before a human ever picks it up
  • Newsletters in Flodesk and hand-coded HTML in Mailchimp, plus DKIM, SPF and DMARC so they reach the inbox
  • Eleven team email signatures with per-source tracking
  • Meta ads driving to the life insurance funnel

What it did

Pending Aaron's sign-off — one life insurance campaign ran 60,022 impressions at 1.20% link CTR and $1.98 a click, returning 24 leads at roughly $59 each, in a vertical where $50 to $100 is normal. Confirm against Amount spent before publishing.

The problem

My own kitchen. Orders arrived as Messenger messages at all hours and I kept losing them between the pan and the phone.

What I built

  • A reservation bot that takes the order and writes it to a sheet
  • The brand, the menu design and the weekly content — the page reached 9.3K followers at 94% recommended
  • Meta ads I ran and paid for myself

What it did, and what it doesn't prove

Two campaigns held 2.51% and 3.54% link click-through across 629,898 impressions. Worth saying plainly: this was food delivery in the Philippines, priced in pesos, and the cost per conversation here will not repeat in a US or Australian market. What travels is the creative discipline that held those click rates at that volume — and the fact I learned it spending my own money, not a client's.

Why these exist
